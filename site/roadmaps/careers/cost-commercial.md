# Cost & Commercial Roadmaps

**Learn how to estimate, budget, track, forecast and manage the commercial side of projects.**

If you want to work in **Cost & Commercial Management**, this page gives you a practical map of the skills used to understand project cost, commitments, contracts and commercial performance.

Cost Management asks:

**How much will the project cost?**

Commercial Management also asks:

**What are we contractually and commercially responsible for, and how do those obligations affect the project?**

A simple way to think about the discipline is:

**Estimate → Budget → Commit → Spend → Measure → Forecast → Control**

---

## What Does a Cost & Commercial Professional Do?

Typical responsibilities include:

- Preparing estimates
- Developing budgets
- Establishing cost structures
- Tracking actual costs
- Monitoring commitments
- Managing accruals
- Forecasting final cost
- Analyzing cost variances
- Supporting procurement
- Reviewing contracts
- Managing commercial changes
- Supporting claims and variations
- Monitoring commercial risks
- Preparing cost and commercial reports

The exact responsibilities vary by organization, contract and industry.

## Understand the Project First

Before managing cost, understand what the project is delivering.

Learn:

- Objectives
- Scope
- Deliverables
- Requirements
- WBS
- Milestones
- Constraints
- Assumptions
- Stakeholders

Cost cannot be controlled effectively if the scope is unclear.

---

## Cost Breakdown Structure

A project needs a structured way to organize cost.

Learn:

- Cost codes
- Cost categories
- Cost accounts
- Cost breakdown structures
- Work packages
- Responsibility

The objective is to answer:

**What does this cost relate to?**

**Where does it belong?**

**Who owns it?**

A consistent cost structure makes reporting and analysis easier.

---

## Estimating

Estimating is the process of developing an expected project cost.

Understand:

- Quantities
- Unit rates
- Labor
- Materials
- Equipment
- Subcontractors
- Overheads
- Allowances
- Contingency
- Assumptions

Common estimating approaches include:

- Analogous estimating
- Parametric estimating
- Bottom-up estimating
- Expert judgment

An estimate is based on assumptions and available information.

It should not be treated as a guaranteed final cost.

---

## Estimate Development

A practical estimating process can be thought of as:

**Scope → Quantities → Resources → Rates → Direct Cost → Indirect Cost → Risk/Contingency → Estimate**

Document important assumptions.

A good estimate should be understandable and traceable.

---

## Budgeting

The estimate becomes part of the project's financial planning.

Learn:

- Budget
- Cost baseline
- Funding
- Time-phased budget
- Cost allocation
- Management reserves
- Approved changes

Understand the difference between:

**Estimate**

and

**Approved Budget**

An estimate is an expected cost.

A budget is an approved financial plan.

---

## Cost Baseline

The cost baseline provides a controlled reference for measuring cost performance.

Understand:

- Planned costs
- Time-phased costs
- Approved budget
- Baseline changes
- Cost variance

A baseline should be controlled and traceable.

Do not change the baseline simply to make performance appear better.

---

## Commitments

Commitments represent costs the project has become obligated to through commercial arrangements.

Examples include:

- Purchase orders
- Subcontracts
- Service agreements
- Other contractual commitments

Track:

**Budget → Commitment → Actual → Forecast**

This provides a better view of the project's financial position.

---

## Actual Costs

Actual costs represent costs recorded as incurred.

Understand:

- Invoices
- Payroll
- Materials
- Subcontractor costs
- Equipment costs
- Other project expenses

Actual cost data often comes from financial or ERP systems.

---

## Accruals

Costs may have been incurred even when the related invoice has not yet been recorded.

Accruals help recognize expected costs associated with work or obligations that have occurred.

Understand:

**Commitment ≠ Actual ≠ Accrual**

These are different pieces of project cost information and should not automatically be treated as interchangeable.

---

## Cost Forecasting

A project needs a forward-looking view of expected final cost.

Learn:

- Estimate to Complete
- Estimate at Completion
- Remaining cost
- Forecast assumptions
- Cost variance
- Forecast trends

A simple concept is:

**Actual Cost + Remaining Forecast = Expected Final Cost**

Forecasts should be updated as project conditions change.

---

## Variance Analysis

Compare:

**Budget → Actual → Forecast**

Then investigate differences.

Ask:

1. What changed?
2. Why did it change?
3. What is the impact?
4. Is it temporary or continuing?
5. What is the forecast?
6. What action is required?

A useful cost report should explain the **reason behind the number**.

---

## Cash Flow

Projects may require cash at different times.

Understand:

- Planned cash flow
- Actual cash flow
- Forecast cash flow
- Payment timing
- Inflows
- Outflows

Connect cash flow with:

- Procurement
- Contracts
- Progress
- Billing
- Payment terms

Cost and cash are related, but they are not the same thing.

---

## Procurement

Procurement can have a major effect on project cost and schedule.

Understand:

**Requirement → Tender → Evaluation → Award → Purchase → Delivery → Payment**

Learn about:

- Procurement planning
- Supplier selection
- Purchase orders
- Subcontracts
- Long-lead items
- Commercial evaluation

---

## Contract Fundamentals

Commercial professionals need to understand the contracts governing project work.

Learn:

- Contract parties
- Scope
- Price
- Payment terms
- Deliverables
- Milestones
- Responsibilities
- Obligations
- Variations
- Notices
- Claims
- Dispute mechanisms

Contract requirements vary by jurisdiction and contract type.

Always understand the actual contract before making commercial decisions.

---

## Contract Types

Become familiar with common commercial structures such as:

- Lump sum
- Unit price
- Cost reimbursable
- Time and materials
- Target cost

Each approach distributes cost and risk differently.

Understand:

**Who carries the risk?**

**How is payment determined?**

**How are changes handled?**

---

## Change & Variations

Project scope and conditions can change.

Learn:

- Change identification
- Variation
- Change request
- Pricing
- Impact assessment
- Approval
- Documentation
- Change register

Assess the effect on:

**Scope + Cost + Schedule + Risk**

Maintain a clear record of approved and pending changes.

---

## Claims

Commercial disputes can arise when parties disagree about contractual entitlement, responsibility, cost or time.

Learn the fundamentals of:

- Notices
- Records
- Entitlement
- Cause
- Effect
- Evidence
- Valuation
- Negotiation
- Resolution

Claims require careful review of the actual contract and project records.

---

## Commercial Risk

Commercial risks can affect both cost and project outcomes.

Examples include:

- Price escalation
- Procurement delays
- Scope changes
- Supplier failure
- Contractual disputes
- Market conditions
- Unclear responsibilities

Connect commercial risks with:

**Cost → Schedule → Cash Flow → Forecast**

---

## Progress & Payment

Commercial performance often depends on measured progress.

Understand:

- Progress measurement
- Valuation
- Payment applications
- Milestones
- Quantity measurement
- Retention
- Payment terms

The exact process depends on the contract.

## Earned Value Connection

## Cost Management vs Commercial Management

These areas overlap but can be distinguished.

### Cost Management

Focuses more heavily on:

**Estimate → Budget → Actual → Forecast → Cost Control**

### Commercial Management

Focuses more heavily on:

**Contracts → Procurement → Obligations → Changes → Entitlement → Commercial Risk**

In many organizations, the responsibilities overlap.

---

## Cost & Commercial vs Project Controls

These disciplines work closely together.

### Project Controls

Focuses broadly on:

**Scope + Schedule + Cost + Progress + Forecast + Performance**

### Cost & Commercial

Focuses more deeply on:

**Estimate + Budget + Cost + Commitments + Contracts + Commercial Performance**

Cost & Commercial therefore forms an important part of integrated project controls.

## Don't Learn Everything at Once

A common beginner mistake is trying to learn:

**Estimating + Contracts + Cost Control + EVM + Claims + Procurement + Power BI**

all at once.

Don't.

Start with:

**Project Fundamentals → Scope → WBS → Estimating → Budgeting**

Then learn:

**Actuals → Commitments → Accruals → Forecasting → Variance**

Finally develop:

**Contracts → Change → Commercial Risk → Claims → Reporting**
